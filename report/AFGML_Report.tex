\begin{abstract}
\noindent We ask whether a budget-aware fallback limits the fairness loss when exact inference in a nine-variable Bayesian network for pre-contact bank outreach is cut off by an operation budget. On UCI Bank Marketing (test $n=8{,}238$), fairness-aware fallback selection reproduces exact decisions (AP $0.421$, $98.3\%$ agreement) but has a larger $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ than the global fallback by $+0.120$ $[0.015,0.226]$; our hypothesis is refuted. Simulation shows group-dependent evidence availability drives fallback disparity. Limits: strong base-rate drift, no customer identifiers, small network.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, context and contribution}
Customers, outreach teams, serving engineers and auditors are affected when a probabilistic scorer runs under a compute limit. $Y=1$: recorded term-deposit subscription; $D=1$: selection for \emph{hypothetical} outreach (top $20\%$ of scores) using only pre-contact information (call \texttt{duration} excluded).\\
\textbf{Research question:} at a fixed operation budget, does choosing the fallback by validation worst-group deviation from the exact decision (AP loss $\le0.01$) yield a smaller $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ than a global fallback, utility-only selection and post-budget threshold adjustment? It is refuted if any Holm-adjusted paired interval contains $0$ or is positive.\\
\textbf{Contribution:} a deterministic operation-count budget with evidence-dependent trips, a simulation isolating group-dependent evidence availability (with a no-dependence control), and the selection rule.\\
\textbf{Closest work:} compute reduction causing group disparity in neural inference~\cite{closest}; our reproduced baseline is group-threshold post-processing~\cite{method}. Eight or more papers are compared in \texttt{docs/related\_work.md}.

\section{Data, graphical model and fairness method}
\textbf{Data and estimand:} \texttt{bank-additional-full.csv}~\cite{data} (41,188 contacts, 4,640 positives, SHA-256 recorded), date-ordered, no customer id; positional 60/20/20 split (24,712/8,238/8,238). Protected attributes: age band ($<30$, $30$--$59$, $\ge60$) crossed with marital status (audit only; not a model input). Groups with $<30$ positives are exploratory. \texttt{unknown} values are unobserved evidence; macro and calendar columns are excluded by declared choice.\\
\textbf{Graph and inference:} a Dirichlet-smoothed (train-only) discrete Bayesian network, no latent variables; $A$ age band, $E$ education, $J$ job, $F$ default, $H$ housing, $L$ loan, $P_d,P_r$ prior-contact bins, $O$ previous outcome:
\[
p(\cdot)=p(A)\,p(E|A)\,p(J|A,E)\,p(F|J)\,p(H|A)\,p(L|H)\,p(P_d)\,p(P_r|P_d)\,p(O|P_r,P_d)\,p(Y|A,J,H,L,O,P_d),
\]
query $P(Y{=}1\mid e)$ with $e$ the observed variables. Exact variable elimination (min-fill, min-weight, min-degree) and clique-tree calibration (treewidth 6) were implemented with factor-entry operation counters.\\
\textbf{Method and fairness:} a query whose exact cost would exceed the budget is answered from a precomputed table (prior, or a variable subset). The proposed rule picks, on validation data, the candidate with the smallest worst-group decision-flip rate relative to exact inference among those within the AP tolerance; routing is group-unaware. Primary criterion $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ (range of group TPRs; a missed responder is the harm); diagnostics $\Delta_{\mathrm{DP}}$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOdds}}$, flips, log loss, Brier. Primary utility: average precision (AP), allowed loss $0.01$. Age band is a model variable and so is used at prediction time; marital status is not.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\textbf{Baselines:} exact inference (reference), global fallback (closest), threshold adjustment after budgeted inference, fair-adjusted exact, logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees on the same variables. Thresholds, tables and selection use train/validation only; the test protocol was frozen beforehand (\texttt{docs/protocol.md}). Budgets are operation counts at quantiles of natural validation cost; the primary $75\%$ quantile is $11$ operations ($258$ at $100\%$); wall-clock is measured separately. Capacity is $0.2$ (robustness $0.1$, $0.3$). Workloads: natural (real \texttt{unknown} pattern, confirmatory) and synthetic group-dependent / group-independent masks (exploratory). Five seeds cover masks, networks and GBT; the natural workload is deterministic. Intervals: fixed-model paired moving-block bootstrap over date-ordered test blocks (length $50$, $1{,}000$ replicates, $95\%$); Holm correction over the three confirmatory comparisons, computed on $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ over the $7$ groups with $\ge30$ positives. Ablations: utility-only selection, global fallback, no evidence-based simplification, generous budget. Robustness: elimination orders, network densities, discretisation, capacities, budgets, masks. Correctness: exact methods agree to $\le2.2\times10^{-16}$ (tolerance $10^{-6}$) on $600$ evidence patterns and unit tests in \texttt{tests/}.

Paired proposed $-$ comparator on $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ (negative favours proposed): global $+0.120$ $[0.015,0.226]$, Holm $p=0.036$; utility-only $-0.294$ $[-0.370,-0.210]$, $p=0.006$; threshold adj.\ $+0.106$ $[-0.006,0.251]$, $p=0.080$.}\\
\textbf{Main finding:} the hypothesis is refuted. Proposed beats utility-only selection, but is worse than the global fallback and indistinguishable from threshold adjustment. The lower $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ of those two reflects levelling down: AP falls by $0.071$ (above the $0.01$ tolerance) and $15$--$18\%$ of decisions change, whereas proposed stays within tolerance ($98.3\%$ agreement). Against exact inference its $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ is lower by $0.042$ $[0.006,0.082]$: no budget-induced degradation, but the $0.465$ gap already exists in the exact model. The BN's AP is below logistic regression ($0.525$).\\
\textbf{Mechanism and failure:} removing fairness-aware selection (A1) raises the worst-group flip rate from $0.038$ to $0.198$, as it picks the age-based fallback. Disabling simplification (A3) raises fallbacks to $26.2\%$, and a generous budget ($258$ operations) restores exact behaviour. In simulated networks the fallback-rate gap is $0.542$ under group-dependent availability versus $0.026$ in the control (difference $+0.516$; each width's interval excludes $0$), yet the $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ effect is uncertain at one width ($+0.069$ $[-0.041,0.179]$). Failure (synthetic dependent masks, exploratory): proposed chose the age-based fallback in $3$ of $5$ seeds (AP loss $0.046$; $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$ $+0.493$ $[0.397,0.569]$ versus exact), while its group-unaware variant lost $0.003$ AP. It also degrades under dense and sparse networks (worst flip $0.206$, $0.133$).

\section{Limitations, ethics and conclusion}
Only the natural \texttt{unknown} pattern is real; masks and networks are simulations. The positive rate drifts across splits ($4.8\%$, $11.1\%$, $30.8\%$), so the validation threshold selects $38.3\%$ of test rows instead of $20\%$. Rows are not distinct customers, so intervals may be too narrow; five groups are exploratory and wide intervals do not show fairness. Contacted-only data say nothing about uncontacted people, and with nine variables exact inference is cheap, so budgets are relative. \textbf{Conclusion:} fairness-aware fallback selection preserves exact decisions at a tight budget but does not give the smallest $\Delta_{\mathrm{EOpp}}$; next, re-run under a re-split that equalises base rates and add worst-group TPR to expose levelling down.
